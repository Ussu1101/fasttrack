Evidence-Based Foundation

The Science of Fasting Biology

Intermittent fasting isn't calorie starvation—it is an evolutionary metabolic switch between nutrient storage and cellular rejuvenation.

science Peer-Reviewed Literature Grounded
recycling Autophagy: The Cellular Clean-Up Crew

In 2016, cell biologist Yoshinori Ohsumi was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology for deciphering autophagy ("self-eating"). When systemic nutrient intake halts for 14–18+ hours, cellular energy sensors (like AMPK) trigger lysosomes to envelop, breakdown, and recycle damaged mitochondria, misfolded proteins, and senescent waste.

stacked_line_chart Insulin & Glycogen

Every meal provokes insulin release to shuttle glucose into muscle and liver cells. Fasting drops basal insulin levels, prompting hormone-sensitive lipase to unlock adipose reserves for energy.

Key Takeaway Fewer insulin spikes allow beta-cells to rest and restore systemic glucose sensitivity.
routine Circadian Digestion

Our gastrointestinal tract, pancreas, and liver operate according to internal molecular clocks. Late-night meals burden digestive organs when melatonin surges, disrupting REM sleep architecture.

Pro Tip Close your feeding window 3 hours prior to sleep for optimum resting heart rate.
warning Frequent Beginner Mistakes to Avoid
Ignoring Electrolytes

When insulin drops, the kidneys excrete sodium. Drink mineral water to stop headaches.

Under-eating Window

Fasting is not severe caloric deficit. Ensure sufficient protein and micronutrient density.

Jumping Straight to OMAD

Train your metabolic flexibility with 14:10 or 16:8 before jumping into prolonged fasts.

Clinical Safety & Medical Disclaimer

Mandatory Notice

FastTrack is an educational and behavioral calculation tool intended solely for healthy adults. Intermittent fasting protocols are strictly not recommended for pregnant or nursing mothers, individuals with a current or prior history of eating disorders, individuals with Type 1 Diabetes, or minors under 18 years of age without close clinical physician supervision. If you are taking prescription blood sugar medications or have chronic renal or cardiovascular conditions, please consult your healthcare physician prior to beginning any fasting schedule.

Clarification & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, scientific answers to your most pressing intermittent fasting questions.

Does black coffee or unsweetened tea break my fast? expand_more

No. Black coffee, plain green tea, and herbal teas contain zero significant caloric energy and do not raise blood glucose or insulin. In fact, polyphenols in black coffee and green tea have been demonstrated in clinical trials to gently stimulate cellular autophagy and promote fat mobilization. Keep it free of sugar, milk, and creamers.

What should I eat to break my fast without stomach distress? expand_more

After an extended fast, your digestive tract is in a quiet, resting state. Break it gently with easily digestible foods: warm bone broth, steamed vegetables with olive oil, hard-boiled eggs, avocado, or a light protein shake. Avoid breaking with ultra-processed sugary pastries or immense high-fat meals which can trigger reactive hypoglycemia and gastric cramping.

Will intermittent fasting cause muscle loss? expand_more

Not when practiced correctly. Fasting stimulates a surge in endogenous Human Growth Hormone (HGH), which acts as a protective shield for lean muscle tissue. As long as you hit your daily targeted protein intake during your eating window and perform regular resistance exercise, muscle retention remains completely preserved.

Can I exercise while in a fasted state? expand_more

Yes. Low-to-moderate intensity aerobic activity (like brisk walking or steady cycling) is optimal in a fasted state because insulin is low and intramuscular fat oxidation is maximized. For high-intensity powerlifting or explosive sprinting, you may choose to time workouts near the start of your feeding window so post-workout recovery nutrients are promptly available.

Can I shift my fasting window on weekends? expand_more

Flexibility is one of intermittent fasting's greatest advantages. You can easily push your window forward by 1-2 hours on weekends to accommodate social dinners or brunches. Simply use FastTrack to recompute your target break-fast timestamp based on when your dinner finishes.
